# Pokémon Dark Rising — experimental export

**Earlier status:** local candidate tables and provisional FireRed-sector reader before the update below. The supplied `.sa1` was entirely `FF` and contains no save. Candidate ROM SHA-256 `8f430dc5324855cef3adf7a208eaca686043a89688980bee2ab73ccde30e2dd1`; no ROM or private data belongs in the repository.

This ROM identifies as FireRed-based, but its species table at `0x245EE0` reuses many Gen 3 internal slots for later Pokémon. The [candidate species/move tables](../../src/PKHeX.Everywhere.RomHacks/Historic/dark-rising-tables.json) identify 190 of 411 species entries as different from standard Gen 3 interpretation or requiring review; 11 names were unresolved by the conservative exact matcher, including form-like and custom names. Twenty-two move IDs had name-matched standard IDs different from their native indices. These are clues, not proof of equivalent forms, move effects, abilities or held items.

The [HistoricGen3 reader](../../src/PKHeX.Everywhere.RomHacks/Historic/HistoricGen3.cs) has now decoded one game-created Dratini party record (native 147, checksum valid) in a fresh in-game save from this ROM; the 100 bytes match the reloaded live EWRAM record at `0x02024284`. The game and party record report level 5 at 135 EXP, but PKHeX calculates level 4 with standard personal data. The growth-curve explanation is plausible, not yet proven. This does not validate PC records or other species. A generic PK3 interpretation can turn native IDs into the wrong species, so do not enable portable export on name matches alone. The requested future policy is generic Ruby/Hoenn origin with an explicitly approximate location and retained native details; exact implementation and PK format remain to be validated. Native ball and shiny encoding are unknown for this revision. Obtain further matching saves/states and compare game-native reads before implementing them.

## Local experimental export update (2026-10-29)

A later-supplied `.sa1` contains all 14 checksum-valid sectors scattered over a 64 KiB, 16-physical-sector flash image. The reader reconstructs this only if sector IDs are complete, unique and from one save counter, then validates every checksum. Its six party records and occupied PC record match the newest supplied VBA 9 state byte-for-byte; four chronological states decoded successfully (1, 3, 6 and 7 records). The earlier blank `.sa1` was from a different folder. No private save or state is included in the repository.

`ClassicRecovered` adds explicitly selected read-only VBA 9 live-party/PC and battery-save export for this exact revision. Portable PK6 uses the ROM-specific candidate name tables for species and moves, so reused species IDs are not treated as retail IDs. The supplied states contain real species, including later-generation mappings; unidentified species and eggs are skipped and unmapped moves retain native-only records. Held items are removed, balls become Poké Balls, shiny, ability and gender use standard assumptions, and source location is replaced by a Hoenn-region approximation. Party EXP may be adjusted to preserve the displayed level; box levels can differ because growth curves can change. Original records and source fields are retained. There is no save-editing support, and browser chooser/download testing is still outstanding. Synthetic tests exercise the scattered-sector reconstruction, incomplete sector rejection, PC pointer checks and original record preservation.

Exact-build mGBA version 7 state extraction is also implemented for party and PC. A fresh game-created starter state matched its saved native party record and portable identity; PC coverage uses synthetic fixtures, not a real mGBA PC. Exact ROM CRC identification is required.
