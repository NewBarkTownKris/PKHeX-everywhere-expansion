# Polished Crystal 3.2.3 — investigation

The supplied normal 3.2.3 ROM loaded the supplied faithful-named save in a private mGBA copy and displayed its existing Continue data. The supplied `.patch` is a Nintendo 3DS Virtual Console configuration text file, not a patch that builds the faithful ROM. The faithful and normal builds can differ in moves and types; loading the save alone does not verify its intended rules. The original save was not modified.

PKHeX Core did not recognize the save as a standard Crystal save. The local PokeDaisy source `scripts/gen_polished_crystal_tables.py` and `Gen2Reader.kt` document a substantially changed format: nine-bit species, dedicated form/gender/egg fields, three DV bytes and changed item/move tables. Real species and supported regional forms need explicit mappings; custom or ambiguous data must be skipped. Live WRAM offsets and the 48-byte party structure are documented there, but the battery save/PC layout, source-version selection, OT names, shiny behavior and portable mapping still need independent verification. PokeDaisy research is credited as a source of analysis; no copied game assets are included. No export support is claimed yet.

## Experimental read-only recovery

The local build supports explicitly selected Polished Crystal 3.2.3 battery saves (normal or faithful) and mGBA live-party states for the exact supplied normal ROM CRC `F98367E4`. State PC extraction and other state ROM revisions are not supported. The faithful-named supplied save’s six party identity/name fields matched the normal ROM’s live state; stats for two party members differed when loaded in the normal variant, so variant mechanics are not asserted equal. Its 82 occupied boxed database entries passed the game’s checksum. The reader recovered 66 portable PK6/PK9 records, retained five mapped records as native-only due to unsupported fields, and skipped 17 unverified alternate forms or other unmapped records. Party names, species and levels matched the game’s party screen; output checksums, trainer IDs, shiny flags and collection archive were checked.

Primary and backup save checksums and markers are validated; damaged primary data falls back to a valid backup. Box records use their independent checksum, including its original weighting and packed name bits; a damaged boxed record is rejected. There are 20 boxes of 20 entries, whose indices reference a split 49-byte Pokémon database across SRAM banks. The source has distinct 9-bit species IDs, altered moves, explicit nature/shiny/ability/gender bits, six four-bit DVs and packed names. Species and move IDs were matched by official names from the public Polished Crystal v3.2.3 source (`constants/pokemon_constants.asm`, `constants/move_constants.asm`, and `constants/charmap.asm`), and save/box structure from its public `ram/sram.asm`, `macros/ram.asm` and `engine/pc/bills_pc.asm` and release symbol file. Credit: [Polished Crystal](https://github.com/Rangi42/polishedcrystal), release v3.2.3; PokeDaisy research provided the live party addresses.

Only `PLAIN_FORM` base species are exported; other forms and eggs are skipped rather than mislabeled. Moves without safe matches remain native-only. A Pokémon’s secret ID and PID are not stored by this game and are generated as explicitly labeled approximations; four-bit DVs become `2*DV+1` IVs. Shiny status and stored nature are preserved. OT gender is approximated, held items are removed, balls become Poké Balls, original location remains in metadata without an invented match, party experience may be adjusted to preserve displayed level, and box PP is estimated. The source-native bytes are retained. No retail legality is implied. Implementation: `Historic/PolishedRecovery.cs` and `Historic/gb-polished-crystal-3-2-3.json`. No game assets or personal save data are included.
